## Motion basis and technique comparison

- `scripts/export_1a_prototype_reference.mjs` extracts the existing MotionExpert reference into `joint-reference.json` (200 frames × 22 XYZ joints). It validates frame/joint counts and finite coordinates. The source sequence is generic pose data, not a named skater's capture.
- `scripts/build_1a_mesh_skates_blender_scene.py` retargets the continuous CesiumMan mesh from those joints and builds the single-file rink scene. Its travel, phase markers, jump lift, root yaw, and playback timing are explicit presentation simulations.
- U.S. Figure Skating describes the counterclockwise Axel as a left forward outside (LFO) take-off and right backward outside (RBO) landing, usually approached from a right backward outside edge with a step to the left forward outside edge; a single Axel is 1.5 revolutions. The scene uses a simulated 540° airborne root turn and a 0.58 s display interval (frames 45–103 at 100 fps). Those settings are chosen to illustrate the description and are not recovered from the MotionExpert sequence.
- The joint sequence does not encode blade edges or verified take-off/landing contacts. The Blender scene therefore does not claim that the source motion proves LFO/RBO contact, measured airtime, a technically judged Axel, or any real competition capture. Every phase and trajectory is marked illustrative/simulated.
- Skate geometry and attachment approach match the existing movement page: a boot is anchored at an estimated ankle landmark and oriented along the ankle-to-foot vector. A parallel-transport frame and symmetric five-frame roll smoothing keep the boot from flipping when the foot axis approaches vertical. Roll is an estimate because the joints contain no measured blade orientation. The skate long axis is re-aligned to the foot vector after roll filtering; the build report records maximum directional error, adjacent-frame rotation step, and blade-grounding residual.

## Rebuild

From the project root, export the joint reference:

```powershell
node scripts/export_1a_prototype_reference.mjs
```

Open the v8 file with Blender 5.2.3. To rebuild it, open the v3 checkpoint and run `scripts/build_1a_human_rink_v8.py` through Blender's Python console or Blender MCP. The builder refuses to overwrite its target path, sets the new scene as the active scene before saving, and verifies that the original default scene remains unchanged. Change the output version before another build.

## Limitations

This is a generic educational visualization, not Beijing 2022 athlete footage or motion capture. Joint order and timing are inherited from the project's existing generic reference; absolute world position is not recorded. The added root turn, rink path, take-off/landing frames, 0.44 m lift, and 1.92 m travel are simulated. Contact edges and judging quality cannot be validated from the available joint positions. The active v8 body and head are project-authored primitives; earlier scenes retained in the `.blend` contain the separately licensed CesiumMan sample mesh. Skate sizing, ankle anchor, and blade roll are visual estimates rather than equipment or athlete measurements. Numeric blade-height continuity was checked for all 200 frames, but every frame was not rendered and visually reviewed.
